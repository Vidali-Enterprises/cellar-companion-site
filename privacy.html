Cellar Companion

Effective: October 18, 2026 · Operator: Vidali Enterprises LLC

About this section

This section explains how Vidali Enterprises LLC handles information in Cellar Companion.

Your account

You can start using the app without supplying an email address; the app creates an account with a unique identifier to keep your cellar.

If you save or open your cellar with an email code, we store your email address for sign-in and send the code through Resend. If you choose Sign in with Apple, Apple supplies an account identifier and the email address you allow it to share, which can be a private relay address instead of your personal address. When Apple supplies your name at first authorization, the app saves it as your display name in the current cellar; you can edit that name in Sharing.

Your sign-in state is also kept on your device so the app can reopen your account.

Your cellar

We store your cellars, their names and members, wine and bottle details, storage racks and positions, saved barcodes, catalogue references and confirmations, and the history of changes to your cellar. Bottle records can include purchase price, currency, date and shop, a consumption date, a rating, and optional notes.

Members of a shared cellar can see its bottles, purchase details, notes, ratings, racks and history, together with members’ display names; this does not give them access to your account email or your other cellars.

Invitations record who created and used them; anyone with a valid invitation code can preview the cellar name, owner’s display name and member count.

The app remembers a cellar’s wine-to-barcode associations even after its last bottle is removed, so a later scan can find that wine again. Those remembered associations are readable only by that cellar’s members.

Shared wine information and community evidence

Wine details are stored in a shared catalogue readable by app accounts: producer, name, vintage, grape, region, colour, estimated drinking window and taste characteristics, with the source of those details. A shared wine entry has no account-owner field; the bottle linking it to your cellar is protected by cellar membership.

When you scan a usable product barcode, the app sends the code and the read’s vintage, or no vintage, to look for community suggestions. After you save a bottle with a usable code, the app attempts to record one confirmation per account, wine and code; repeated saves by the same account do not add to that count.

Other app accounts can read the barcode-to-wine association, vintage, number of distinct confirming accounts, and first and last confirmation times, together with the shared wine details. They cannot read the private confirmation ledger that links a vote to an account and bottle. These counts provide suggestions for you to choose, not automatic confirmation of a wine.

A wine edit can replace its barcode vote, and deleting the contributing bottle or account removes the corresponding votes and updates the community counts.

What stays on your device

Label photos and wine-list photos are read on your device and are not uploaded by the app. Text recognition, label reading and matching against the bundled wine reference run on the device, without sending the photos or raw recognized text to an AI service or the reference provider. The wine fields you save from a read are part of the cellar records described above and do sync to the server.

Temporary capture copies are deleted after reading and review copies are discarded when the confirmation flow closes; a saved bottle can keep a small label thumbnail locally on your phone. The thumbnail is not synced to the cellar service and may be included in your phone’s own backup.

Storage and service providers

Supabase hosts account authentication and cellar storage in the United States, in its Oregon region (us-west-2). Resend receives the destination email address and sign-in message to deliver email codes. Apple handles Sign in with Apple and the authorization used to revoke that sign-in access at account deletion.

The app uses these records to save and reopen your cellar, share it with its members, and provide the community suggestions described above.

Deleting your account

In the app, open Settings and choose Delete account to remove your account, its sign-in information, memberships and barcode votes, and every cellar you own with its bottles, racks, remembered wine associations, invitations and history. If other people still belong to a cellar you own, you must transfer its ownership or remove those members before deleting your account.

Bottles and history you contributed to other people’s cellars remain there with your actor identity removed and displayed as a former member. Shared wine catalogue entries remain because they do not identify an account owner.

For an Apple-linked account, deletion first asks you to authorize with Apple so our server can exchange that authorization and revoke Apple sign-in access in the same request, without storing Apple’s returned credentials. Cancelling the Apple sheet leaves your account unchanged. If revocation fails, you can retry once; after a second failure you can still delete your account, and the app explains how to remove the sign-in under Settings → Apple Account → Sign in with Apple.
